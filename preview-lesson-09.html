Soft Gel Tips
& Proper Sizing

A good fit begins with observation—not guesswork. Learn to choose tips that follow the natural nail comfortably and consistently.

Learn. Practice. Perfect.

Understand the tip. Check the fit. Record every size.

01 · YOUR LEARNING GOALS

By the end of this lesson…

  • Explain what a full-cover soft gel tip is.
  • Distinguish full-cover tips, partial-well tips and sculpted extensions.
  • Check width, sidewall coverage, curvature and contact.
  • Recognise a tip that is too narrow, too wide or poorly matched.
  • Pre-size and record all ten tips individually.
  • Explain why product compatibility and manufacturer instructions matter.
02 · UNDERSTAND THE OPTIONS

Three ways to create length

These extension methods are different. Their fit, contact area, materials and application steps are not interchangeable.

Full-cover soft gel tip

Pre-shaped tip covers most or all of the nail plate using its compatible adhesive system.

Partial-well tip

Attaches at a smaller contact area near the free edge and is finished according to its system.

Sculpted form

Enhancement product is built over a form positioned beneath the natural free edge.

Remember: a tip that looks beautiful in the box is not automatically the right fit for a particular nail.
03 · ASSESS BEFORE YOU SELECT

Read the natural nail first

Observe the nail before comparing any tip. Look at its natural contours and condition without forcing or pressing on it.

  • Overall width and sidewall shape.
  • Transverse curve across the nail.
  • Length and condition of the free edge.
  • Whether the nail is relatively flat, curved or asymmetrical.
  • Surrounding skin and any visible damage or irritation.
If the nail is injured or shows a concerning change, postpone the service and follow appropriate professional guidance.
04 · WIDTH & SIDEWALL COVERAGE

Choose the correct width

A suitable tip should cover the intended nail area from sidewall to sidewall without pressure, pinching or rocking.

TOO NARROW

Edges may remain uncovered. Do not stretch or force the tip.

TOO WIDE

May overhang. Tailor only if the manufacturer permits it.

FIT CHECK

Coverage follows the nail contours without pinching or rocking.

Tip numbers vary by brand and range. Compare the actual tip to the nail—not the number alone.
05 · CURVATURE & CONTACT

Width is only one part of fit

Check whether the tip's transverse curve and interior contact area suit the natural nail.

  • Does the tip follow the nail's curve naturally?
  • Does it sit evenly rather than rock side to side?
  • Does the contact area suit the nail's shape?
  • Would the mismatch create a gap or pressure point?
Never press hard to force a match or use adhesive to fill a large mismatch.
06 · LENGTH, SHAPE & COMFORT

Choose a suitable finish

Length and shape should suit the natural nail, the client's everyday activities, the service goal and the selected product system.

Short

Practical length option; check fit and shape before application.

Medium

Consider the requested shape and how added length changes leverage.

Long

Plan carefully; length should never override comfort or structure.

Do not promise a fixed wear time. Retention depends on the system, application, client habits and aftercare.
07 · PREPARATION BEFORE APPLICATION

Pre-size all ten tips

Complete the fit checks before opening adhesive or beginning application. Each natural nail must be assessed individually.

Compare each tip with its matching natural nail.
Check width, sidewall coverage and curvature.
Tailor only if the tip maker allows it.
Recheck the fit after any permitted tailoring.
Arrange tips in finger order for each hand.
Record size numbers and tailoring notes.
Do not assume matching fingers on the left and right hands use the same size.
08 · RECOGNISE THE WARNING SIGNS

Common sizing mistakes

Choosing by number alone

Sizes differ between brands. Compare the actual tip with the nail.

Stretching a narrow tip

Pressure and poor sidewall coverage are signs to reassess the size.

Ignoring the curve

A width match can still have unsuitable contact or curvature.

Over-filing a wide tip

Excessive tailoring can weaken or distort the tip. Follow the maker's limits.

Sizing only one hand

Check every nail individually; natural nails can differ.

Filling gaps with adhesive

Adhesive is not a substitute for selecting a compatible tip.

When the fit is uncertain: pause, reassess, and choose a suitable tip or system. Never trade comfort for speed.
09 · HANDS-ON LEARNING

Practical activity: the ten-tip fit map

Use a practice hand or non-injured practice tips. This activity is about observing, comparing and recording—not applying product.

Choose a soft gel tip set and read its fitting instructions.
Size all ten practice nails without adhesive.
Photograph each hand from above and front/end.
Mark nails where width, curve or coverage is uncertain.
Tailor only where permitted, then recheck.
Record sizes for both hands and explain one rejected fit.

10 · PROFESSIONAL CHECKLIST

Before moving to application

  • Every tip has been individually sized.
  • Sidewall coverage is appropriate.
  • Curvature and contact are compatible.
  • No tip needs to be forced or held under pressure.
  • Any tailoring follows the manufacturer's instructions.
  • All ten tips are organised and recorded.
  • The tip, adhesive and lamp are a compatible system.
KEY TAKEAWAY

Fit first. Speed later.

Correct sizing begins with observing the natural nail. A well-chosen tip follows its width and contours without force.

Safety reminder
Uncured gel must not contact skin. Follow the exact product instructions for preparation, adhesive, curing and removal. Use the lamp specified or approved for the system.
