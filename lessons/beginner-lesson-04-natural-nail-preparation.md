# Beginner Lesson 4 — Natural Nail Preparation

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to explain the purpose of natural nail preparation; inspect the nails and surrounding skin before a service; shape the free edge safely; distinguish non-living cuticle from living skin; prepare the nail plate gently; and follow the product system's cleansing and preparation instructions.

> This is introductory education, not a substitute for supervised practical training. Follow local rules, product labels and manufacturer instructions. Never diagnose a nail or skin condition.

## Page 1 — Why preparation matters
Natural nail preparation creates a clean, suitable foundation for the chosen service. Good preparation supports neat application and product performance, but it should never damage the natural nail or surrounding skin.

The goal is not to remove layers of the nail. It is to remove surface residue, manage the free edge, gently address non-living cuticle on the nail plate where appropriate, and prepare the surface exactly as the product system requires.

**Remember:** gentle, consistent preparation is better than aggressive filing.

## Page 2 — Assess before you begin
Before touching tools or products:
- Ask about sensitivities, allergies, recent reactions and relevant service history.
- Observe the natural nail and surrounding skin for cuts, broken skin, swelling, unusual discolouration, pain or other concerns.
- Check for product residue or an existing enhancement that requires a separate removal procedure.
- Confirm the requested service is suitable and explain any limitations.

If the nail or surrounding skin appears injured, inflamed, painful or potentially infected, do not work over it. Pause or decline the service and recommend that the client seek advice from an appropriate healthcare professional. Do not diagnose.

## Page 3 — Hand hygiene and workstation preparation
Prepare a clean workstation before the client sits down. Separate clean tools and supplies from used items. Ensure your tools have been processed appropriately and that single-use supplies are new.

Perform hand hygiene before and after each client and at the appropriate points during the service. Ask the client to wash or sanitise their hands using a suitable method. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.

Keep products closed until needed. Have clean wipes, a suitable dust brush and waste container ready. Follow the hygiene and disinfection process required in your location and by each product label.

## Page 4 — Shape the free edge
Use a suitable file and light, controlled strokes to refine the free edge. Choose an abrasive appropriate for natural nails and the task. Avoid sawing aggressively, forcing the file into the sidewalls or creating heat.

Work with the client's preferred shape while respecting the natural nail's length and condition. Check both hands for consistent shape and length. If a nail is weak, split or painful, do not force it into a shape that could worsen the damage.

Never use a file to thin the natural nail plate.

## Page 5 — Understand the cuticle area
The cuticle is non-living tissue that may remain attached to the nail plate as it grows. The eponychium is living skin at the proximal nail fold and helps form a protective seal.

Where appropriate and within your training, gently move back the proximal fold only enough to reveal non-living cuticle attached to the nail plate. Use a suitable tool with minimal pressure. Do not cut living skin, scrape aggressively, dig under the fold or continue if the client feels pain.

If you cannot clearly distinguish non-living cuticle from living tissue, leave it alone. A safe service does not require aggressive cuticle removal.

## Page 6 — Remove non-living residue gently
If the product system and your training call for it, use an appropriate method to lift or remove only non-living cuticle residue from the nail plate. Keep tools at a controlled angle and use light pressure.

Do not use sharp tools to dig into the nail folds. Do not scrape until the nail plate looks excessively white, thin or sensitive. Stop if there is discomfort, redness, broken skin or bleeding.

The natural nail plate should remain intact. Preparation is not a race and more filing does not mean better adhesion.

## Page 7 — Refine the nail plate safely
Some gel systems require gentle removal of surface shine; others specify a different preparation method. Follow the exact instructions for the product being used.

If light buffing is required, use the recommended abrasive, minimal pressure and only the amount of work specified. Do not repeatedly buff the same area. Never use a coarse file to remove layers of the natural nail.

Remove loose dust with a clean, appropriate brush or lint-free wipe. Keep the brush clean and do not transfer dust or residue between clients.

## Page 8 — Cleanse and prepare for product
Use the cleanser, dehydrator or primer only when required by the chosen system. Apply each product as directed, using the stated amount and avoiding contact with skin.

Do not touch the prepared nail plate with fingers after cleansing. Keep the surface free from dust, oils, lotion and other contamination. If the nail is touched or contaminated, repeat only the appropriate system-approved preparation step.

Products from different systems are not automatically compatible. Do not substitute household alcohol, acetone or other liquids for a specified professional product unless the manufacturer confirms it is suitable.

## Page 9 — Practice activity and knowledge check
### Practice activity
On a practice hand or your own natural nails, rehearse the preparation sequence without applying product:
1. Inspect the nail and surrounding skin.
2. Prepare the workstation and perform hand hygiene.
3. Refine the free edge with light strokes.
4. Identify the difference between living proximal nail-fold skin and non-living cuticle on the plate.
5. Demonstrate a gentle, controlled approach without cutting or scraping living tissue.
6. Explain which cleanser or preparation products your chosen gel system requires.

### Quiz
1. What is the main purpose of natural nail preparation? **Answer:** To create a clean, suitable foundation without damaging the natural nail or skin.
2. Should you continue if the surrounding skin is broken or bleeding? **Answer:** No. Stop or decline the service and follow appropriate hygiene and first-aid procedures.
3. What is the cuticle? **Answer:** Non-living tissue that may remain attached to the nail plate.
4. Should you aggressively buff the natural nail to improve adhesion? **Answer:** No. Follow the system's instructions and use only gentle preparation if required.
5. What determines which cleanser, dehydrator or primer to use? **Answer:** The instructions and compatibility requirements of the chosen product system.

### Completion takeaway
Assess first. Work gently. Protect living tissue. Follow the product system. A healthy natural nail matters more than aggressive preparation.
