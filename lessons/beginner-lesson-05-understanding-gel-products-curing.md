# Beginner Lesson 5 — Understanding Gel Products & Curing

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to identify common gel product categories; explain the basic purpose of base coat, colour gel and top coat; understand the difference between curing and air-drying; identify factors that affect curing; follow lamp and product compatibility instructions; and handle uncured gel carefully.

> This is introductory education, not a substitute for supervised practical training. Follow local rules, product labels and manufacturer instructions. Never use a lamp, product combination or curing time that the manufacturer has not approved.

## Page 1 — What makes gel different?
Gel products are formulated to harden through a light-activated chemical process called curing. They do not simply dry by evaporation like regular nail polish. The product must receive the correct light exposure under the conditions specified by its manufacturer.

A gel service depends on the complete system: the gel product, the compatible lamp, the application method and the specified curing time. A product that feels dry or firm on the surface is not proof that it has cured completely throughout.

**Remember:** curing is a chemical process, not just a drying step.

## Page 2 — Common gel product categories
Different products have different jobs:
- **Base coat:** designed to form the foundation between the prepared nail and the following gel layers, when required by the system.
- **Colour gel / gel polish:** provides colour and may be applied in multiple thin layers as directed.
- **Top coat:** provides the specified finish, such as glossy or matte, and protects the design.
- **Builder or structure gel:** a thicker product designed for added strength, overlay or extension work, depending on its formulation.
- **Gel adhesive / tip gel:** products made for specific extension or soft-gel-tip systems.

Names alone do not guarantee that two products are interchangeable. Some systems require a particular base, top coat or bonding product.

## Page 3 — UV and LED lamps
Nail lamps emit specific wavelengths of light to activate photoinitiators in compatible gel products. The words “UV” and “LED” describe lamp technologies, but the label alone does not confirm compatibility with every gel.

Check the manufacturer's stated lamp requirements, wavelength range, power or model guidance, and curing time. A lamp that appears bright or powerful is not automatically suitable for a particular gel.

Do not assume that any LED lamp will cure any gel, or that a longer exposure can compensate for an incompatible lamp. Use the lamp and gel combination specified or approved by the manufacturer.

## Page 4 — What affects curing?
Curing can be affected by several factors:
- The gel formulation and its compatibility with the lamp.
- The specified wavelength and the lamp's condition.
- The thickness of each applied layer.
- Pigment density, especially in very dark or highly opaque colours.
- Correct hand placement and exposure for the full instructed time.
- Lamp age, damage, dirt or performance changes.

Apply gel in the layer thickness recommended by the manufacturer. Keep the product away from the skin and make sure the fingers are positioned correctly inside the lamp. Do not shorten the curing time to save time.

## Page 5 — Thin layers and careful application
Thick layers can prevent light from reaching all of the product evenly. Apply thin, even coats and cure each layer exactly as instructed. Highly pigmented shades may have specific application guidance.

Avoid flooding the sidewalls or cuticle area. If gel touches the skin, remove it completely before curing, using the appropriate method and without spreading it. Never cure gel while it remains on the skin.

Do not rely on wiping the surface or checking whether it feels hard to decide if curing is complete. Follow the system instructions every time.

## Page 6 — Uncured gel and skin safety
Uncured gel can contain reactive ingredients that may cause skin sensitisation. Repeated or direct skin contact increases risk. An allergy can develop even after previous services without an obvious reaction.

- Avoid all skin contact with uncured gel.
- Do not touch, wipe or handle uncured product with bare skin.
- Remove accidental skin contact promptly and safely before curing, following product guidance.
- Do not allow clients to place hands in the lamp with gel on their skin.
- Stop the service if a client reports burning, itching, pain or an unusual reaction.

Do not diagnose or attempt to treat a reaction. Refer clients with concerning symptoms to an appropriate healthcare professional.

## Page 7 — Heat sensations and client comfort
Some gel products can release heat during curing. A strong heat sensation may occur for several reasons, including product formulation, layer thickness, nail condition and curing conditions.

Follow the manufacturer's directions for application and curing. Never tell a client to tolerate pain or burning. If a client experiences discomfort, have them remove their hand from the lamp and follow the product maker's guidance before deciding whether the service can safely continue.

Do not invent a “low heat” setting or change curing steps unless the product and lamp instructions specifically allow it.

## Page 8 — Product storage and system compatibility
Keep containers closed when not in use. Store products according to their labels, away from direct sunlight, excessive heat and contamination. Keep product rims and brushes clean, and avoid mixing products from different systems unless compatibility is confirmed by the manufacturers.

Check product names, labels, batch or expiry information where provided, and instructions before use. Discontinue products that have changed in a way the manufacturer identifies as unsafe or unsuitable.

A professional result starts with correct product selection, clean handling and following the complete system instructions.

## Page 9 — Practice activity and knowledge check
### Practice activity
Using product labels and manufacturer instructions, create a simple reference card for one gel system:
1. Identify the base, colour, top and any structure products.
2. Record the compatible lamp model or requirements.
3. Record the instructed curing time for each product layer.
4. Note the recommended layer thickness and any special colour guidance.
5. Identify the steps for avoiding and removing accidental skin contact.
6. Explain why you must not substitute an unapproved lamp or shorten curing time.

### Quiz
1. Do gel products air-dry like regular nail polish? **Answer:** No. They harden through a light-activated curing process.
2. Does every LED lamp cure every gel? **Answer:** No. The lamp and gel must be compatible according to manufacturer instructions.
3. Why should gel be applied in the recommended thin layers? **Answer:** To help light reach the product as intended and support proper curing.
4. Should gel be cured while touching the client's skin? **Answer:** No. Remove all skin contact before curing.
5. Can you shorten curing time because the surface feels hard? **Answer:** No. Follow the full specified curing instructions.

### Completion takeaway
Know the product. Match the lamp. Apply thin layers. Prevent skin contact. Follow the manufacturer's curing instructions every time.
