# Beginner Lesson 2 — Nail Hygiene, Sanitation & Disinfection

## Learning outcomes
Students will explain cleaning, sanitising and disinfecting; prepare a hygienic workstation; distinguish reusable tools from single-use items; describe safe tool processing; prevent cross-contamination; and recognise when to stop a service.

> This is general education, not a substitute for local regulations, product labels, safety data sheets or tool-manufacturer instructions. Follow the rules that apply where you practise.

## Page 1 — Why hygiene matters
Nail services involve close contact with hands, skin and natural nails. A consistent hygiene routine helps protect clients and technicians and supports a professional service.

The hygiene cycle: prepare the workstation; perform hand hygiene; check the service area; use appropriately processed tools; keep clean and used items separate; process reusable tools; discard single-use items; reset the station.

**Remember:** A beautiful result is never more important than health and safety.

## Page 2 — Cleaning, sanitising and disinfecting
**Cleaning** removes visible dust, dirt, product residue and other material, usually with detergent and water or a suitable cleaner.

**Sanitising** reduces microorganisms to a level considered safer under an applicable standard. Exact definitions and requirements vary by setting.

**Disinfecting** uses an appropriate product or process to inactivate specified microorganisms on compatible surfaces or tools when used exactly as directed.

Clean first. Residue can interfere with disinfection. Disinfectant is not a substitute for cleaning.

## Page 3 — Hand hygiene and personal presentation
Perform hand hygiene before and after each client, after handling used tools or waste, after removing gloves, after restroom use, and whenever hands are visibly dirty.

Use soap and running water when hands are visibly soiled. Use an appropriate hand rub only when suitable and according to its directions. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.

Keep hands and nails clean. Cover cuts with a suitable waterproof dressing. Do not work when you may expose clients to a contagious condition; follow local health guidance.

## Page 4 — Prepare the workstation
Clean the work surface, then use an appropriate surface disinfectant when required. Follow the product's label and full contact time. Set out only what is needed, keep processed tools protected, use a clean barrier where appropriate, and place a lined waste bin within reach.

Keep food, drinks and personal belongings away from the service area. Use chemicals with suitable ventilation and never spray toward clients, electrical equipment or open products.

## Page 5 — Reusable tools and single-use items
Compatible reusable, non-porous tools must be cleaned and disinfected using a process approved for that item and use.

Single-use or porous items—such as wooden sticks and disposable files or buffers intended for one client—must be discarded after use. Do not attempt to disinfect and reuse them.

A tool is not safe just because it looks clean. Never return used tools to the clean-tool area. If an item cannot be properly processed, do not reuse it.

## Page 6 — Safe tool processing
Keep dirty and clean items separate. Wear appropriate protective equipment. Safely remove debris, clean according to the tool instructions, rinse and dry if required, then disinfect using the correct product, dilution and contact time.

Use immersion only when the product label permits it. Rinse after disinfection only if directed. Dry completely and store in a clean, closed or protected area.

Never mix chemicals, guess dilution or shorten contact time. If the label is missing or unreadable, do not use the product until instructions are confirmed. Local rules may require a higher level of processing for certain tools or exposures.

## Page 7 — Prevent cross-contamination
Cross-contamination transfers microorganisms or contaminants from one person, object or surface to another.

Keep clean and used tools separate; never share disposable items; use a clean spatula for shared product pots where appropriate; do not re-dip used applicators; keep phones away from the clean work area; clean spills promptly; and replace or correctly reprocess anything that becomes contaminated.

## Page 8 — When to stop or decline a service
Do not perform a service over open wounds, active bleeding, or skin that appears infected or significantly inflamed. Do not diagnose.

Pause, explain calmly that you cannot safely proceed, avoid touching or treating the affected area, and recommend advice from an appropriate healthcare professional. If blood or body fluid contaminates a tool or surface, follow applicable exposure-control and public-health procedures. Do not improvise.

## Page 9 — Practice activity and quiz
### Practice activity
On a practice station, identify clean and used-tool areas; prepare the surface; separate reusable tools from single-use supplies; explain hand-hygiene moments; demonstrate how to locate and follow a real disinfectant label; and identify where used items are discarded.

### Quiz
1. What should happen before disinfecting a reusable tool? **Answer: Clean it to remove visible soil and residue.**
2. Can a single-use file be reused after spraying it? **Answer: No, discard it after use.**
3. What determines dilution and contact time? **Answer: The product label and applicable requirements.**
4. What should you do if the client has an open wound in the service area? **Answer: Stop or decline the service and recommend appropriate professional advice.**
5. Why keep clean and used tools separate? **Answer: To prevent cross-contamination.**

### Completion takeaway
Clean first, disinfect correctly, respect single-use items, separate clean from used tools, and never compromise safety for a service.
