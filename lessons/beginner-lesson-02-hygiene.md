# Beginner Lesson 2 — Nail Hygiene, Sanitation & Disinfection

**Naileditby_gel Learn · Learn. Practice. Perfect.**

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, the student should be able to:
- Explain the difference between cleaning, sanitising and disinfecting.
- Prepare a clean workstation before and after each client.
- Identify which items are reusable and which must be single-use.
- Follow safe handling, cleaning and disinfection procedures for reusable tools.
- Reduce cross-contamination risks during a nail service.
- Recognise when a service must not proceed.

> **Important scope note:** This lesson teaches general salon hygiene principles. Always follow local public-health requirements, product labels, safety data sheets, and the instructions of the tool and disinfectant manufacturers. Requirements can differ by jurisdiction and product.

---

## Page 1 — Why hygiene matters

A nail service brings the technician into close contact with a client's hands, skin and natural nails. Good hygiene helps reduce the spread of microorganisms and protects both the client and technician.

A professional service begins before any product is opened. The workstation, hands, tools, products and waste-handling routine all matter.

**Remember:** A beautiful result is never more important than health and safety.

### The hygiene cycle
1. Prepare the workstation.
2. Perform hand hygiene.
3. Check the client's nails and surrounding skin.
4. Use clean, appropriately processed tools and supplies.
5. Prevent clean items from touching used items.
6. Clean and process reusable tools after the service.
7. Dispose of single-use items safely.
8. Reset the workstation for the next client.

## Page 2 — Cleaning, sanitising and disinfecting

These words are related, but they do not mean the same thing.

### Cleaning
Cleaning removes visible dirt, dust, product residue and other material, usually with water and detergent or an appropriate cleaner. Cleaning is an essential first step because residue can interfere with disinfection.

### Sanitising
Sanitising reduces the number of microorganisms to a level considered safer under the applicable standard. The term and required process can vary by setting and local rules.

### Disinfecting
Disinfection uses an appropriate chemical product or process on compatible, non-porous items and surfaces to inactivate specified microorganisms when used exactly as directed.

**Key rule:** Disinfectant is not a substitute for cleaning. Clean first, then disinfect when the item and procedure require it.

## Page 3 — Hand hygiene and personal presentation

Perform hand hygiene:
- Before and after every client.
- After touching used tools, waste or contaminated surfaces.
- After removing gloves.
- After using the restroom, eating or handling personal items.
- Whenever hands are visibly dirty.

Use soap and running water when hands are visibly soiled, and follow public-health guidance for handwashing technique. An appropriate hand rub may be used when hands are not visibly dirty and the product directions allow it.

Keep nails and hands clean. Cover cuts with a suitable waterproof dressing and use gloves when indicated by the task and applicable guidance. Gloves do not replace hand hygiene.

Avoid working when you may expose clients to a contagious condition. Follow local health guidance and seek professional medical advice when needed.

## Page 4 — Prepare the workstation

Before the client sits down:
- Clean the work surface to remove dust and residue.
- Apply an appropriate surface disinfectant if required; follow its label and contact time.
- Allow the surface to remain visibly wet for the full label-specified contact time, where required.
- Set out only the tools and supplies needed for the service.
- Keep processed tools protected until use.
- Place a clean, disposable or freshly laundered barrier where appropriate.
- Position a lined waste bin within easy reach.
- Keep food, drinks and personal belongings away from the service area.

Do not spray chemicals toward a client, electrical equipment or open products. Use products in a well-ventilated area and follow their safety instructions.

## Page 5 — Reusable tools and single-use items

### Reusable, non-porous tools
Examples may include compatible metal implements and certain hard plastic tools. They must be cleaned and then disinfected using a product and process approved for that tool and use.

### Single-use or porous items
Examples commonly include wooden sticks, disposable files, buffers and toe separators when labelled or intended for single use. Discard after one client; do not attempt to disinfect and reuse them.

### Important distinctions
- A tool is not safe merely because it looks clean.
- Do not place used tools back with clean tools.
- Do not assume every metal tool can be processed in the same way; follow manufacturer and local requirements.
- If an item cannot be properly cleaned and processed, do not reuse it.

## Page 6 — Safe tool processing

Use a dedicated, organised process that keeps dirty and clean items separate.

1. Put on appropriate protective equipment for the task.
2. Place used reusable tools in a designated, labelled container.
3. Remove visible debris safely and clean the tools according to their instructions.
4. Rinse and dry if the cleaning product or process requires it.
5. Fully immerse or otherwise process the tool only if the disinfectant label specifically allows that method.
6. Use the correct dilution, fresh solution where required, and the full contact time on the label.
7. Rinse after disinfection only if the product directions require it.
8. Dry completely and store in a clean, closed, labelled container or protected area.

Never mix disinfectants or chemicals. Never guess the dilution or shorten the contact time. If the label is missing or unreadable, do not use the product until its instructions are confirmed.

**Not all tools can be disinfected to the level required for every use.** Follow local rules for any item that may contact broken skin or blood; ordinary surface disinfectant may not be sufficient.

## Page 7 — Prevent cross-contamination

Cross-contamination happens when microorganisms or contaminants are transferred from one person, object or surface to another.

Avoid it by:
- Washing or sanitising hands at the correct moments.
- Keeping clean and used tools in separate, clearly identified areas.
- Never re-dipping a used applicator into a shared product container.
- Using a clean spatula to remove product from shared pots when appropriate.
- Never sharing disposable files, buffers or wooden sticks between clients.
- Cleaning spills promptly using the correct procedure.
- Avoiding contact between product containers and the client's skin.
- Keeping phones and personal items away from the clean work area.

If a tool or supply becomes contaminated, stop and replace or reprocess it correctly before continuing.

## Page 8 — When to stop or decline a service

Do not perform a nail service over open wounds, active bleeding, or skin that appears infected or significantly inflamed. Do not diagnose a condition.

If you notice an unusual change, pain, swelling, discharge, broken skin or another concern outside your training:
1. Pause the service.
2. Explain calmly that you cannot safely proceed.
3. Avoid touching or attempting to treat the affected area.
4. Recommend that the client seek advice from an appropriate healthcare professional.
5. Clean and process anything that may have been exposed, following the correct procedure.

If blood or body fluid contaminates a tool or surface, follow the applicable exposure-control and local public-health procedure. Do not improvise.

## Page 9 — Practice activity and knowledge check

### Practice activity: workstation reset
Using a practice station (not a client), demonstrate the following:
1. Identify the clean-tool area and used-tool area.
2. Prepare the work surface.
3. Arrange single-use items separately from reusable tools.
4. Explain when hand hygiene is needed.
5. Describe the correct steps for cleaning and disinfecting one compatible reusable tool using its actual product label.
6. Show where used single-use items will be discarded.

### Five-question quiz

**1. What should happen before disinfecting a reusable tool?**
- A. Put it straight into the disinfectant
- B. Clean it to remove visible soil and residue
- C. Wipe it on a towel

**2. Can a disposable file intended for single use be reused after spraying it?**
- A. Yes, if it looks clean
- B. Yes, for the same client next week
- C. No, discard it after use

**3. What determines the correct disinfectant dilution and contact time?**
- A. The technician's preference
- B. The product label and applicable requirements
- C. How busy the salon is

**4. What should you do if a client has an open wound in the service area?**
- A. Cover it with gel product
- B. Continue carefully
- C. Stop or decline the service and recommend appropriate professional advice

**5. Why should clean and used tools be kept separate?**
- A. To prevent cross-contamination
- B. To make the station look larger
- C. To save product

### Answer key and explanations
1. **B** — Cleaning removes residue that can interfere with disinfection.
2. **C** — Single-use items must be discarded after use.
3. **B** — Follow the label and local requirements; never guess.
4. **C** — Do not work over open wounds or attempt to treat a condition.
5. **A** — Separation prevents contaminants from reaching processed tools.
