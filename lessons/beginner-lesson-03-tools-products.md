# Beginner Lesson 3 — Nail Tools, Equipment & Products

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to identify essential manicure tools and equipment; explain their purposes; distinguish reusable and single-use supplies; select appropriate products for a basic gel-polish service; and store and maintain tools safely.

> Follow the instructions and safety information supplied by each product and equipment manufacturer. This lesson is introductory and does not replace hands-on training or local requirements.

## Page 1 — Set up your professional toolkit
A nail technician does not need every tool on the market. Begin with a small, reliable set of tools that suit the service you offer. Choose good-quality equipment, keep it clean and organised, and learn the correct purpose of each item before using it.

Your kit should support safe preparation, application, finishing, cleaning and storage. Avoid buying products simply because they are trending.

## Page 2 — Core manicure tools
**Nail clippers/nippers:** Designed for specific trimming tasks. Use only as intended and never cut living skin.

**Nail files:** Shape the free edge. Grit indicates abrasiveness; use a suitable grit and light pressure for the task. Do not aggressively file the natural nail plate.

**Buffer:** Gently refines a surface when appropriate. Over-buffing can thin and weaken the natural nail.

**Cuticle pusher:** Used carefully to move only non-living cuticle from the nail plate when appropriate. Never force it under living skin or use painful pressure.

**Dust brush:** Removes loose filing dust. Keep it clean and do not share contaminated brushes between clients without appropriate processing.

## Page 3 — Equipment and workstation essentials
A stable table, comfortable chair, suitable task lighting, dust-control arrangements, covered storage and a lined waste bin help create an organised workstation.

An electric file is an optional specialist tool, not a beginner requirement. It requires proper training, compatible bits, safe speed and pressure control, and careful avoidance of the natural nail and skin.

Choose equipment that can be cleaned and maintained according to its manufacturer. Keep cords, lamps and electrical devices in good condition and away from liquids.

## Page 4 — Gel products: what each one does
**Nail cleanser:** Used for a specific cleaning step where the system directs.

**Dehydrator:** Temporarily removes surface moisture and oil when required by the product system.

**Primer/bonder:** Supports adhesion when recommended. It is not automatically needed for every system.

**Base coat:** The foundation layer used under gel colour when required by the system.

**Gel polish:** A formulated coating that must be applied and cured according to its instructions.

**Top coat:** The finishing layer that seals and protects the colour or design.

Products from different systems are not automatically compatible. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and do not mix systems unless compatibility is confirmed.

## Page 5 — Lamps and curing safety
Use the lamp type and wavelength range specified for the gel product. A lamp that turns on is not proof that it cures every gel correctly.

Follow the stated application thickness, curing time, lamp positioning and maintenance instructions. Keep the lamp clean and replace or service it as directed.

Avoid skin contact with uncured gel. Repeated skin exposure can cause sensitisation or allergic reactions. If gel touches skin, remove it before curing using the method specified by the product manufacturer. Do not cure product on skin.

## Page 6 — Files, abrasives and disposables
Files and buffers come in different grits and materials. Select one suitable for the task and use light, controlled strokes. Never use an abrasive so harshly that it heats, hurts or thins the natural nail.

Understand which items are single-use and discard them after the client. Reusable items must be compatible with the required cleaning and disinfection process. If you cannot safely process an item, do not reuse it.

## Page 7 — Product storage and safe handling
Keep products in their original, labelled containers with lids closed. Store them according to the manufacturer's temperature, light and ventilation directions. Keep them away from heat, flames, children and pets.

Read labels and safety data sheets. Do not use expired, contaminated, separated or unidentified products. Never pour products into unlabelled containers or mix chemicals. Dispose of product waste according to local requirements.

## Page 8 — Choosing a beginner-friendly kit
Before buying, ask:
- Is this tool necessary for the services I offer?
- Can I clean or safely dispose of it as required?
- Is the product labelled and from a reliable supplier?
- Do I have the correct lamp and instructions for this gel system?
- Can I store it safely?
- Do I know how to use it without damaging the natural nail?

Start with essentials and add specialist equipment only after training.

## Page 9 — Practice activity and quiz
### Practice activity
Arrange a practice kit into five groups: preparation tools, application products, curing equipment, finishing tools and hygiene/disposable supplies. Explain the purpose of each item and identify how it is cleaned, stored or discarded.

### Quiz
1. What does a nail file primarily shape? **Answer: The free edge.**
2. Should you aggressively buff the natural nail? **Answer: No, over-buffing can thin and weaken it.**
3. What determines which lamp to use with a gel? **Answer: The gel manufacturer's specified lamp requirements.**
4. Why avoid skin contact with uncured gel? **Answer: It can contribute to sensitisation and allergic reactions.**
5. What should you do with an unlabelled or unidentified product? **Answer: Do not use it.**

### Completion takeaway
Know your tools, use products as directed, protect the natural nail, and keep equipment clean and safely stored.
