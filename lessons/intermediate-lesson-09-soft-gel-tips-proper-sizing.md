# Intermediate Lesson 9 — Soft Gel Tips & Proper Sizing

**Course:** Intermediate Nail Education  
**Tagline:** Learn. Practice. Perfect.  
**Status:** Draft for technical review — not published  
**Scope:** Tip selection, fit assessment and preparation for a soft-gel full-cover tip service. This lesson does not replace the exact instructions supplied with a chosen tip and adhesive system.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:
1. Explain what a full-cover soft gel tip is and distinguish it from a partial-well plastic tip or a sculpted extension.
2. Select a tip that fits the natural nail from sidewall to sidewall without forcing it.
3. Assess the tip's curvature, contact area, length and sidewall alignment.
4. Recognise signs of an undersized, oversized or poorly matched tip.
5. Explain why product compatibility, preparation and manufacturer instructions matter.
6. Plan and record tip sizes consistently for future appointments.

> **Safety note:** Follow the exact tip and adhesive manufacturer's instructions. Do not assume that all soft gel tip systems use the same preparation, etching, adhesive, flash-cure or full-cure steps. Uncured gel must not contact skin. Use the lamp specified or approved for the system.

## Page 1 — What is a soft gel full-cover tip?

A full-cover soft gel tip is a pre-shaped enhancement that covers most or all of the natural nail plate. It is designed to be attached using the compatible adhesive or gel system. It differs from a traditional partial-well tip, which is attached at a smaller contact area, and from a sculpted extension, which is built using product and a form.

Soft gel tips come in different shapes, lengths, widths, curves and interior contact designs. A tip that looks attractive in the box is not automatically suitable for a particular natural nail.

**Remember:** The goal is a comfortable, stable fit that follows the natural nail's contours—not simply choosing a number that looks close.

**Visual direction:** Three-panel comparison: full-cover soft gel tip, partial-well tip, and sculpted extension on a form. Use clear labels and neutral studio lighting.

## Page 2 — Read the natural nail before choosing

Before selecting a tip, observe the natural nail:
- overall width and sidewall shape;
- transverse curve and how strongly the nail curves across its width;
- length and condition of the natural free edge;
- whether the nail plate is relatively flat, curved or asymmetrical;
- surrounding skin and any visible damage or irritation.

Do not force a rigid tip onto a nail whose natural curve does not match. A mismatch can create pressure, gaps or uneven contact. If the nail is injured or shows a concerning change, postpone the service and follow appropriate professional guidance.

**Visual direction:** Overhead and front/end views of several different natural nail shapes, with arrows indicating width, sidewalls and transverse curve. Label the images as examples, not diagnostic categories.

## Page 3 — Choose the correct width

A correctly sized tip should cover the nail from sidewall to sidewall without being forced down or creating pressure. Check the fit while the tip is resting naturally in place.

- **Too narrow:** May leave natural nail edges uncovered or require pressure to make the tip sit.
- **Too wide:** May overhang the sidewalls and require careful tailoring if the manufacturer permits it.
- **Better fit:** Covers the intended area and follows the nail contours without pinching or rocking.

Some manufacturers advise choosing the larger size and tailoring the lateral edges when between sizes. Others may specify a different method. Follow the chosen system's guide rather than applying one rule to every brand.

**Visual direction:** Three matched close-ups labelled TOO NARROW / TOO WIDE / FIT CHECK. Show sidewall coverage clearly; avoid misleading anatomy.

## Page 4 — Check curvature and contact

Width alone does not guarantee a good fit. Check the tip's transverse curve and interior contact area:
- Does the tip follow the natural nail's curve without needing to be flattened?
- Does it sit evenly, rather than rocking from side to side?
- Does the contact area suit the natural nail's shape?
- Is there a visible mismatch that would create a gap or pressure point?

Do not press hard to force a match. If the curvature is unsuitable, select a different tip shape or system. Never use adhesive to fill a large mismatch or conceal poor fit.

**Visual direction:** Side-by-side front/end views showing compatible curvature versus an obvious mismatch. Add a small side view to show rocking or uneven contact.

## Page 5 — Length, shape and comfort

Select a tip length and shape that suit the client's natural nail, lifestyle, service goal and the product system. Consider how length changes leverage and daily use. A dramatic shape should not override fit or comfort.

Check:
- the free-edge length requested;
- whether the tip's pre-formed shape can be refined as permitted;
- whether both sidewalls remain covered after tailoring;
- whether the finished design will remain suitable for the client's needs.

Explain realistic maintenance expectations. Do not promise a fixed wear time; retention depends on the system, application, client habits and aftercare.

**Visual direction:** A neat shape-and-length board showing short/medium/long examples, with almond, square and coffin silhouettes. Keep the nail size consistent enough to compare shape.

## Page 6 — Pre-size all ten tips

Before opening adhesive or beginning application, size all ten nails and arrange the selected tips in finger order.

1. Compare each tip to its matching natural nail.
2. Check width, sidewall coverage and curvature.
3. Tailor only if the tip maker allows it, removing small amounts evenly.
4. Recheck the fit after tailoring.
5. Arrange tips from thumb to little finger for each hand.
6. Record the size numbers and any tailoring notes for future reference.

Do not assume the same size fits corresponding fingers on both hands. Natural nails can differ.

**Visual direction:** Overhead workstation photograph with ten selected tips arranged in two rows, labelled left and right hand. Include a simple size-record card.

## Page 7 — Common sizing mistakes

**Choosing by number alone:** Size numbers vary by brand and product range. Compare the actual tip to the nail.

**Selecting a narrow tip and stretching it:** This can create pressure and poor sidewall coverage. Reassess the size.

**Ignoring curvature:** A width match can still have a poor contact fit.

**Over-filing a wide tip:** Excessive tailoring can weaken or distort the tip. Follow the product maker's limits.

**Sizing only one hand:** Each nail must be checked individually.

**Using adhesive to hide gaps:** Adhesive is not a substitute for selecting a compatible tip.

**Visual direction:** A troubleshooting grid with one image per mistake and a short “recheck fit” cue.

## Page 8 — Practical activity: the ten-tip fit map

Use a practice hand or your own non-injured practice tips:
1. Select a soft gel tip set and read its fitting instructions.
2. Size all ten practice nails without adhesive.
3. Photograph each hand from above and the front/end.
4. Mark any nail where width, curvature or sidewall coverage is uncertain.
5. Tailor only where the product instructions allow, then photograph again.
6. Complete a tip-size record for left and right hands.
7. Explain why you rejected at least one unsuitable tip, if applicable.

Do not practise unfamiliar application or curing techniques on a paying client.

**Client communication practice:** “I'll check each tip against your natural nail before application. If a tip does not follow your nail's shape comfortably, I'll choose or tailor a more suitable option rather than force it.”

## Page 9 — Professional checklist and takeaway

Before proceeding to application, confirm:
- each tip is individually sized;
- sidewall coverage is appropriate;
- curvature and contact are compatible;
- no tip needs to be forced or held under pressure to fit;
- tailoring follows the tip manufacturer's instructions;
- all ten tips are organised and recorded;
- the selected adhesive, tip and lamp belong to a compatible system.

**Key takeaway:** Correct sizing begins with observing the natural nail. A well-chosen tip should fit its width and contours without force. Never trade comfort or compatibility for speed.

## Knowledge check

**1. What is the main purpose of sizing a full-cover tip?**  
A. Choose the smallest number available.  
B. Find a tip that follows the nail's width and contours without force.  
C. Make every finger use the same size.  
**Correct: B.** The actual fit matters more than the number printed on the tip.

**2. What should you do if a tip rocks because its curve does not match the nail?**  
A. Press harder until it sits.  
B. Fill the mismatch with extra adhesive.  
C. Reassess and choose a more compatible tip or system.  
**Correct: C.** Do not force a mismatch or use adhesive to hide it.

**3. Why should all ten nails be sized before application?**  
A. To organise the service and check each nail individually before adhesive is used.  
B. Because both hands always use identical sizes.  
C. To skip the fit check during application.  
**Correct: A.** Pre-sizing helps prevent rushed choices and keeps tips organised.

**4. If a tip is slightly wide, what is appropriate?**  
A. File it aggressively until it is thin.  
B. Tailor it only if the manufacturer permits, then recheck the fit.  
C. Push it down harder.  
**Correct: B.** Tailoring must preserve the tip and follow its system instructions.

**5. What is the best response to a tip that leaves a sidewall uncovered?**  
A. Continue because adhesive will cover it.  
B. Choose a more suitable size or shape and recheck.  
C. Ignore the gap if the colour is opaque.  
**Correct: B.** Sidewall fit is part of selecting a suitable tip.
