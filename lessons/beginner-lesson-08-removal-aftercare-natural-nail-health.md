# Beginner Lesson 8 — Removal, Aftercare & Natural Nail Health

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to explain why removal must follow the product system's instructions; distinguish safe product removal from picking, peeling or forceful scraping; identify signs that mean a service should stop; give practical aftercare advice; and recognise when a client should seek appropriate healthcare guidance.

> This lesson is introductory education, not a substitute for supervised practical training. Always follow local regulations, product labels and manufacturer instructions. Never diagnose or treat a nail or skin condition.

## Page 1 — Protecting the natural nail
Healthy-looking nails depend on careful services over time. The aim of removal is to take off the enhancement or coating without unnecessarily thinning, scraping or injuring the natural nail and surrounding skin.

The natural nail can be weakened by aggressive filing, picking, peeling, forceful scraping, repeated over-preparation or using a removal method that is not suitable for the product.

**Remember:** removal is a technical service, not a race. Preserve the natural nail rather than trying to remove product as quickly as possible.

## Page 2 — Identify the product before removal
Different gel systems have different removal requirements. Some products are designed to soak off using a specified remover; others are file-off or hard-gel systems and should not be treated as soak-off products.

Before starting:
1. Identify the exact product and system used, where possible.
2. Read the manufacturer's removal instructions.
3. Prepare the correct tools, remover and protective supplies.
4. Explain the process to the client and check for discomfort or concerns.
5. Stop if the product cannot be identified and you cannot confirm a safe removal method.

Never assume all gel products dissolve in acetone. Do not improvise a removal method.

## Page 3 — Safe removal principles
Follow the product maker's sequence and timing. Use only the tools and materials intended for that system. Keep filing controlled and avoid filing into the natural nail.

For products specified as soak-off, use the recommended remover and method. Do not exceed the manufacturer's guidance or force product that has not released. For file-off systems, remove product carefully while maintaining a safe margin above the natural nail, using appropriate training and technique.

Never pull, pry, peel or lever off attached product. Do not aggressively scrape the nail plate. If removal is not progressing as expected, pause and reassess the product instructions rather than applying more force.

## Page 4 — When to stop a removal service
Stop the service if the client reports pain, burning or unusual sensitivity, or if you notice broken skin, bleeding, swelling, unusual discoloration, suspected infection or a possible allergic reaction.

Do not file over injured or irritated areas. Do not cover a concerning area with fresh product. Do not diagnose the cause or attempt to treat it. Explain calmly that the service needs to stop and encourage the client to seek appropriate healthcare advice for concerning or persistent symptoms.

If product or remover causes unexpected irritation, follow the product safety instructions and local workplace procedures.

## Page 5 — Clean-up and natural nail assessment
After removal, gently remove dust and residue using the method recommended for the product. Do not buff aggressively to make the nail look artificially smooth or shiny.

Assess the natural nail without scraping or probing. Note visible changes such as unusual thinning, splitting, tenderness or discoloration. Explain what you observe in neutral language and avoid making medical claims.

If the nail or surrounding skin appears damaged or concerning, postpone further enhancement and recommend appropriate professional healthcare advice where needed. Record relevant service notes according to your studio's privacy practices.

## Page 6 — Aftercare clients can follow
Give clients clear, realistic advice:
- Do not pick, peel, bite or forcefully remove product.
- Do not use nails as tools to open, scrape or pry objects.
- Keep nails and surrounding skin clean; dry hands thoroughly after washing.
- Apply a suitable hand moisturiser and cuticle oil to intact surrounding skin as part of routine care.
- Avoid cutting or aggressively pushing living skin around the nail.
- Follow the recommended maintenance or removal schedule for the product system.
- Contact the technician if lifting, damage or unexpected discomfort occurs; do not glue down or cover a problem area themselves.

Aftercare should support comfort and reduce avoidable damage. It cannot guarantee that a reaction or nail problem will not occur.

## Page 7 — Common removal mistakes
**Peeling off product:** this can remove layers of the natural nail. Use the correct removal method instead.

**Using one method for every gel:** products differ. Identify the system and follow its instructions.

**Filing until all colour disappears:** excessive filing can thin the natural nail. Work carefully and maintain a safe margin.

**Scraping forcefully:** attached product should never be pried off. Stop and reassess if it does not release as expected.

**Reapplying over damage or irritation:** do not conceal a problem with more product. Stop and advise appropriate next steps.

## Page 8 — Practical activity and client communication
### Practice activity
On a training tip or practice hand, not on an injured or irritated natural nail:
1. Read a product maker's removal instructions.
2. Identify whether the example product is soak-off or file-off.
3. Arrange the correct tools and materials for that system.
4. Rehearse the sequence on the practice material without forceful scraping or peeling.
5. Practise explaining aftercare in simple, non-medical language.
6. Write down two reasons you would stop a removal service.

### Client communication example
"Today we'll use the removal method specified for your product. If you feel pain, burning or unusual discomfort, please tell me so I can stop and check. I won't force product off the nail. If I notice anything that makes the service unsuitable, I'll explain it and recommend that you get appropriate advice."

## Page 9 — Final takeaway
Identify the product. Follow its removal instructions. Never peel or force attached product. Protect the natural nail from aggressive filing and scraping. Stop when there is pain, injury or a concerning change. Finish with practical aftercare advice and refer health concerns to an appropriate healthcare professional.

**Completion takeaway:** safe removal protects the natural nail today and supports healthier service decisions in the future.
