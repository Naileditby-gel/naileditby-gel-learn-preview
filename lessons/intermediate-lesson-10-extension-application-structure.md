# Intermediate Lesson 10 — Extension Application & Structure

**Course:** Intermediate Nail Education  
**Tagline:** Learn. Practice. Perfect.  
**Status:** Draft for technical review — not published  
**Scope:** Understanding extension systems, planning a suitable extension, and learning the checkpoints in a controlled application workflow. Exact product steps and cure schedules must come from the chosen manufacturer's instructions.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:
1. Distinguish common extension methods: full-cover tips, partial-well tips and sculpted forms.
2. Select an extension method appropriate to the natural nail, desired length and product system.
3. Explain the role of preparation, fit, support, product control and curing.
4. Recognise common application faults such as gaps, bubbles, product on skin and uneven alignment.
5. Describe a safe, methodical workflow without treating one brand's procedure as universal.
6. Inspect a finished extension and identify when to stop, correct or seek further instruction.

> **Safety note:** This is educational theory and a practice framework, not a substitute for supervised hands-on training. Follow the exact product, adhesive, form and lamp manufacturer's instructions. Never cure uncured gel on the skin or assume one cure time applies to all systems.

## Page 1 — What is an extension?

A nail extension adds length beyond the natural free edge. The length may be created using a pre-formed tip or by sculpting product over a nail form. Different systems use different materials, attachment methods and removal procedures.

The extension method should suit the client's natural nail, the desired length and shape, the technician's training and the product system. The longest extension is not automatically the best result.

**Remember:** A successful extension is planned, fitted and finished as part of a complete structure—not simply attached to the fingertip.

**Visual direction:** Three-part comparison showing a full-cover soft gel tip, a partial-well tip and a sculpted form extension. Use matching angles and clear labels.

## Page 2 — Choose the method

**Full-cover soft gel tip:** A pre-shaped tip covers most or all of the nail plate and is attached with its compatible adhesive system. Fit and contact must be checked before application.

**Partial-well tip:** A tip is attached at a smaller contact area near the free edge, then blended and overlaid according to the system's instructions.

**Sculpted form:** A disposable or reusable form is positioned beneath the free edge, and enhancement product is built to create the extension.

These methods are not interchangeable. Their preparation, contact area, product requirements, shaping and removal can differ.

**Visual direction:** A labelled side-by-side diagram showing where each method connects to or extends from the natural nail.

## Page 3 — Plan before application

Assess the natural nail and agree on the service:
- natural nail condition and free-edge length;
- desired extension length and shape;
- natural nail width and curve;
- suitability of the chosen product and method;
- client's everyday activities and maintenance expectations;
- any visible damage or irritation that means the service should be postponed.

Explain the realistic result and aftercare. Do not promise a fixed wear time or continue over an unsuitable nail.

**Visual direction:** Consultation board with natural nail observations, length/shape swatches and a simple service-planning card.

## Page 4 — Preparation and fit checkpoints

Preparation must follow the chosen system. In general, the technician should work with clean hands and tools, prepare the natural nail only as directed, remove dust and use only the compatible preparation products.

Before attaching a tip or placing a form:
- confirm the tip size and contact fit, or the form's fit and alignment;
- check that sidewalls are not forced or pinched;
- ensure a form sits securely without an unintended gap;
- confirm the extension is aligned with the finger's centreline;
- arrange the materials so the application can be controlled.

Do not copy a preparation sequence from another brand. Some systems require different primers, adhesives, etching methods or base products.

**Visual direction:** Two workstations: tip-fit checkpoint and sculpting-form fit checkpoint. Show the key fit areas with arrows.

## Page 5 — Controlled product placement

Use only the product and application method specified for the chosen system. Keep product placement controlled and avoid flooding the surrounding skin or cuticle area. For adhesive-based full-cover systems, follow the manufacturer's method for contact coverage and air removal. For sculpted systems, build the extension according to the product's training guidance.

If a tip shifts, a form moves, a gap appears or air becomes trapped, stop and correct the setup before curing or continuing. Do not cure a visibly compromised application just to save time.

**Visual direction:** A close-up sequence of controlled placement shown as an educational illustration: align, lower or place according to system, inspect contact, pause if a bubble or gap is visible. Caption that exact technique is system-specific.

## Page 6 — Curing and product compatibility

Gel curing depends on the formulation, layer thickness, lamp output, wavelength and the manufacturer's validated system. A lamp that physically fits the hand is not automatically compatible with every gel.

- Use the lamp specified or approved by the product manufacturer.
- Follow the exact cure schedule and application thickness.
- Do not rely on surface appearance to decide whether gel is fully cured.
- Keep uncured product off the skin.
- If the client experiences unusual heat or discomfort, follow the product safety guidance and stop as appropriate.
- Do not layer incompatible products or mix systems unless compatibility is confirmed by the manufacturers.

**Visual direction:** A compatibility diagram linking product system → approved lamp → instructed cure schedule. Avoid displaying universal seconds.

## Page 7 — Refine and inspect

After the system's required curing and cleansing steps, refine only as directed. Check:
- alignment with the finger;
- shape and symmetry from above;
- side profile and transitions;
- free-edge thickness and finish;
- product around the cuticle and sidewalls;
- whether the natural nail has been protected.

Do not file aggressively to disguise a poorly fitted extension or to force a particular structure. If the extension is unstable, painful or visibly compromised, stop and reassess rather than applying more product.

**Visual direction:** A four-view inspection board: top, side, front and underside, with restrained callouts for alignment, transition, sidewalls and free edge.

## Page 8 — Common application problems

**Gap between tip and natural nail:** Recheck size, curvature and the system's contact requirements. Do not fill a major mismatch with extra adhesive.

**Air pocket or bubble:** Stop and follow the product system's reapplication instructions. Do not simply cure over a visible trapped bubble.

**Tip or form is off-centre:** Reposition before proceeding, according to the system instructions.

**Product touches skin:** Remove uncured product safely before curing, following the product's safety directions. Do not cure product on skin.

**Excessive bulk or uneven shape:** Reassess the application and refine only within safe product and natural-nail limits.

**Premature lifting or detachment:** Investigate fit, preparation, compatibility, curing and aftercare; do not assume one cause.

## Page 9 — Practice workflow and professional takeaway

### Practice activity

On a training hand or practice tip:
1. Compare the three extension methods and choose one for the practice scenario.
2. Read the exact manufacturer's guide for the selected system.
3. Prepare the practice setup and check tip fit or form placement.
4. Rehearse the system's application sequence without using a paying client.
5. Inspect the result from top, side and front views.
6. Record one strength, one issue and the correction or next learning step.

### Final checklist

- The method suits the natural nail and requested service.
- Tip fit or form placement has been checked.
- Product and lamp are compatible.
- The manufacturer's exact preparation and cure instructions are followed.
- Uncured product has not been cured on skin.
- The extension is aligned and inspected from multiple views.
- Any concern is addressed before the service continues.

**Key takeaway:** Extension quality begins before product application. Good planning, correct fit, controlled placement, compatible curing and careful inspection all matter.

## Knowledge check

**1. Which statement best describes the difference between a full-cover tip and a sculpted form?**  
A. They are the same method.  
B. A full-cover tip is pre-shaped and attached; a form supports product while an extension is sculpted.  
C. A form is always glued over the whole nail.  
**Correct: B.** The methods have different contact and construction approaches.

**2. What should be checked before attaching a tip or placing product on a form?**  
A. Fit, alignment and compatibility with the chosen system.  
B. Only the colour of the product.  
C. Whether the client wants the longest possible nail.  
**Correct: A.** Fit and setup affect the whole application.

**3. What should you do if a visible air pocket remains under a full-cover tip?**  
A. Cure it anyway.  
B. Add colour to hide it.  
C. Stop and follow the system's correction or reapplication instructions.  
**Correct: C.** Do not knowingly cure a compromised fit.

**4. Can one lamp cure every gel product for the same amount of time?**  
A. Yes, all lamps and gels are identical.  
B. No; use the lamp and cure schedule specified or approved for the product system.  
C. Any bright light is suitable.  
**Correct: B.** Curing is system-dependent.

**5. What should happen if uncured gel touches the skin?**  
A. Cure it immediately.  
B. Wipe or remove it safely according to product instructions before curing.  
C. Cover it with top coat.  
**Correct: B.** Do not cure uncured product on skin.
