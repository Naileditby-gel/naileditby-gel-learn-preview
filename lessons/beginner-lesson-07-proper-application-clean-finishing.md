# Beginner Lesson 7 — Proper Application and Clean Finishing

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to plan a clean gel polish service sequence; control product placement around the cuticle area and sidewalls; refine the free edge without damaging the natural nail; apply compatible finishing products correctly; inspect the finished service; and explain safe client aftercare.

> This lesson is introductory education, not a substitute for supervised practical training. Follow local rules, product labels and manufacturer instructions. Use only compatible products and lamps. Avoid all skin contact with uncured gel.

## Page 1 — What makes an application professional?
A professional result is more than a smooth, glossy surface. It combines careful preparation, controlled product placement, correct curing, clean edges, a balanced finish and safe client care.

The aim is to create a neat enhancement or gel polish service while preserving the health of the natural nail and surrounding skin. Do not sacrifice safety to make a line look closer to the cuticle.

**Remember:** clean finishing begins before the first product is applied.

## Page 2 — Plan the service sequence
Before beginning, review the client's nail condition, agreed service and product system. Confirm that the service is appropriate to proceed. Pause if there is broken skin, pain, swelling, unusual discoloration or a possible infection; do not diagnose the concern.

Prepare a clean workstation and arrange products in their order of use. Check labels, expiry information where provided, product compatibility and the lamp specified by the manufacturer. Keep containers closed when not in use.

A consistent sequence reduces missed steps:
1. Hand hygiene and client consultation.
2. Nail assessment and preparation.
3. Product application in the system's required order.
4. Correct curing of each layer.
5. Finishing, inspection and aftercare advice.

## Page 3 — Controlled product placement
Use an amount of product that you can control with the brush. Work in smooth, deliberate strokes and maintain a safe margin from the cuticle area and sidewalls.

Do not push gel onto the skin or flood the proximal or lateral nail folds. Product touching skin must be removed completely before curing, following the manufacturer's safe removal guidance. Never cure gel while it remains on the skin.

If product repeatedly floods the edges, reduce the amount on the brush, adjust the brush angle and work in smaller sections. Do not solve the problem by pressing harder or moving product onto the skin.

## Page 4 — Keep the cuticle area and sidewalls neat
A clean cuticle line is created through preparation and brush control, not by applying product against living skin. Approach the proximal area with a controlled brush and leave the safe margin required by the system.

Check both sidewalls from more than one viewing angle. Rotate the client's finger gently if needed, while keeping the hand stable and comfortable. Before curing, inspect the full perimeter for gel, base coat or top coat that may have touched the skin.

If you cannot safely remove accidental contact, stop and follow the product maker's guidance. Do not cure over it.

## Page 5 — Shape and refine the free edge safely
The free edge should look even and suit the agreed shape and length. Use an appropriate file with controlled, light strokes. Support the finger and avoid filing into the surrounding skin or aggressively thinning the natural nail.

Refine the side edges without digging into the lateral folds. Keep the file angle consistent and check the shape from the top, front and side. Do not use force to correct a shape; reassess your angle and technique.

If the service system requires sealing or capping the free edge, follow the manufacturer's instructions. Not every product or technique uses the same finishing method.

## Page 6 — Top coat and final curing
Choose the compatible top coat specified for the system. Apply a thin, even layer, covering the intended surface and following any instructions for the free edge. Keep product away from skin and remove accidental contact before curing.

Position the hand correctly in the compatible lamp. Cure for the exact time and conditions specified by the manufacturer. Do not shorten curing because the surface looks glossy or feels firm.

Some top coats leave a tacky inhibition layer; others are no-wipe. Remove residue only when instructed, using the specified cleanser and lint-free material. Do not wipe a no-wipe top coat unnecessarily.

## Page 7 — Final inspection and quality checklist
Inspect the finished nails in good light before the client leaves. Check:
- Product is not touching the cuticle area or surrounding skin.
- Sidewalls and free edges look neat and even.
- The surface is smooth and consistent for the chosen product.
- There are no visible bubbles, debris, pooling or obvious missed areas.
- The product has been cured using the correct system and process.
- The client is comfortable and has no unexpected pain or irritation.

If you find a defect, correct it only using the product maker's approved procedure. Do not cover uncured product, lifting or contamination with another layer.

## Page 8 — Common finishing problems and client care
**Uneven shape:** reassess the file angle and compare the nail from several views.

**Rough or bulky free edge:** use the system-approved refinement method without thinning or damaging the natural nail.

**Top coat on skin:** remove it completely before curing; never cure product on skin.

**Dull or uneven finish:** check product compatibility, application thickness, curing conditions and whether the product requires a specific cleansing step.

**Possible reaction or discomfort:** stop the service. Do not diagnose or attempt to treat a reaction. Encourage the client to seek appropriate healthcare advice for concerning or persistent symptoms.

Explain aftercare clearly: avoid picking or peeling product, do not use nails as tools, follow the recommended maintenance interval and contact the technician if lifting, damage or an unusual reaction occurs. Clients should not glue down or reapply product over a problem area themselves.

## Page 9 — Practical activity and knowledge check
### Practice activity
On a practice hand or suitable training tips:
1. Set up a clean workstation and check product and lamp compatibility.
2. Rehearse a controlled application while keeping product off the skin.
3. Inspect the cuticle area and both sidewalls before curing.
4. Refine the free edge with light, controlled filing strokes.
5. Apply the compatible top coat and cure as directed.
6. Inspect the finished result from the top, front and side.
7. Record two strengths and one improvement for your next practice.

### Completion takeaway
Plan the sequence. Control the brush. Keep product off the skin. Refine gently. Cure exactly as directed. Inspect every nail before finishing the service.
