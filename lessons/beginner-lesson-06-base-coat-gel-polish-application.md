# Beginner Lesson 6 — Base Coat & Gel Polish Application

## Learning outcomes
By the end of this lesson, students should be able to explain the role of a base coat; prepare a clean application setup; apply compatible base and colour gel in controlled thin layers; manage the cuticle and sidewall margins; cure each layer according to the product system; identify common application mistakes; and maintain safe, hygienic working habits.

> This is introductory education, not a substitute for supervised practical training. Follow local rules, product labels and manufacturer instructions. Use only compatible products and lamps. Avoid all skin contact with uncured gel.

## Page 1 — The purpose of a base coat
A base coat is the foundation layer used in many gel polish systems. It is designed to prepare the surface for colour and support the performance of the complete system. Some products are formulated as base coats, while others are combined base-and-colour products. Always check the product label.

A base coat is not a substitute for correct nail preparation. Dust, oil, moisture, contamination or damaged nail surfaces can affect the service. Follow the exact preparation steps required by the gel system.

**Remember:** use the base product specified for the system; do not assume every base coat is interchangeable.

## Page 2 — Set up for controlled application
Before applying product, confirm that the workstation is clean and organised. Complete hand hygiene, check the client's nails and surrounding skin, and ensure tools and single-use supplies are ready.

Arrange the products in the order you will use them. Keep lids closed when products are not in use. Check labels, product condition and expiry information where provided. Confirm that the lamp is clean, functioning and approved for the products being used.

Do not proceed over broken skin, pain, swelling or signs that require the service to be paused. Do not diagnose a nail or skin concern.

## Page 3 — Applying base coat
Use the brush to pick up a controlled amount of base coat. Apply a thin, even layer over the nail plate, following the system's instructions. Keep a small, consistent margin around the cuticle area and sidewalls so product does not touch the skin.

Avoid flooding the proximal nail fold or sidewalls. If product touches the skin, remove it completely before curing, following the manufacturer's safe removal guidance. Never cure gel while it is on the skin.

Some base coats require a specific application technique or may leave a tacky inhibition layer. Do not wipe or alter the surface unless the system instructions say to do so.

## Page 4 — Correct hand placement and curing
After applying the base coat, position the hand correctly in the compatible lamp. Fingers should be placed as instructed so the light reaches the product as intended. Thumbs may require separate positioning in some lamps.

Cure for the exact time specified by the product manufacturer. Do not shorten the time, guess based on appearance or use an unapproved lamp. If the hand shifts or a finger is outside the effective curing area, follow the manufacturer's instructions for correcting the exposure.

A surface that feels firm is not proof of complete curing. Use the approved product-and-lamp combination and the specified process every time.

## Page 5 — Applying gel colour in thin layers
Pick up a small, controlled amount of colour gel. Apply a thin coat with smooth strokes, keeping the product away from skin and avoiding heavy pooling at the cuticle or sidewalls.

Thin layers help light reach the product as intended and support more even curing. Highly pigmented or opaque colours may require particular layer thickness or application guidance. Follow the product instructions rather than trying to cover the nail in one thick coat.

If more coverage is needed, apply additional thin layers and cure each one as directed. Do not allow uncured layers to contact the skin.

## Page 6 — Neat cuticle area and sidewalls
A neat application begins with control, not with pushing product against the skin. Use a suitable brush angle and a small amount of product to approach the cuticle area while maintaining a safe margin.

Keep the sidewalls clean and avoid flooding the lateral folds. Check the nail from different angles before curing. If there is accidental skin contact, remove it completely before the hand enters the lamp.

Do not use a finger, bare skin or an unsuitable tool to wipe uncured gel. If you cannot remove accidental contact safely, stop and follow the manufacturer's guidance rather than curing over it.

## Page 7 — Common application problems
Common beginner issues include:
- **Flooded cuticle area:** too much product or a brush placed too close to the skin.
- **Uneven coverage:** inconsistent brush pressure or an overloaded brush.
- **Thick layers:** trying to achieve full opacity in one coat.
- **Product on sidewalls:** poor hand positioning or insufficient control.
- **Premature lifting:** possible causes include inadequate preparation, contamination, incompatible products or application outside the system instructions.
- **Wrinkling or incomplete cure:** may be associated with excessive thickness, incorrect lamp compatibility or curing conditions.

Do not cover a problem with another layer. Identify the likely cause and follow the product maker's correction or removal instructions.

## Page 8 — Finishing, checks and client care
After the final colour layer has been cured, apply the compatible top coat if required by the system. Cure it for the instructed time. If the top coat leaves a tacky layer, remove it only with the specified cleanser and lint-free material.

Inspect the finished nails for skin contact, uneven edges, pooling, missed areas or signs of discomfort. Do not file or refine in a way that damages the natural nail or compromises the product system.

Explain appropriate aftercare and remind the client to contact you if they experience an unusual reaction. Do not diagnose or attempt to treat a reaction; advise them to seek appropriate healthcare guidance for concerning symptoms.

## Page 9 — Practice activity and knowledge check
### Practice activity
On a practice hand or suitable training tips, rehearse a controlled gel polish application without rushing:
1. Set up a clean workstation and check product and lamp compatibility.
2. Prepare the nail according to the selected system.
3. Apply a thin base coat while keeping product off the skin.
4. Position the hand correctly and cure for the specified time.
5. Apply a thin colour layer, keeping the cuticle and sidewalls clean.
6. Cure each layer as directed; repeat thin layers only if needed.
7. Apply the compatible top coat and complete the final cure.
8. Inspect the result and identify one area to improve.

### Completion takeaway
Control the amount. Keep gel off the skin. Use thin layers. Position the hand correctly. Follow the exact curing instructions for every layer.
