Preventing Lifting
& Troubleshooting

Learn to recognise common causes of lifting, identify warning signs early and respond with a careful, system-led approach.

Learn. Practice. Perfect.

Prepare correctly. Apply with control. Investigate before you correct.

01 · YOUR LEARNING GOALS

By the end of this lesson…

  • Recognise early signs and common patterns of lifting.
  • Explain how preparation, product control and curing affect adhesion.
  • Identify service, product and lifestyle factors that may contribute.
  • Choose a safe response when lifting or damage is found.
  • Know when to stop, remove product safely or refer for professional advice.
Lifting has more than one possible cause. Assess the whole service rather than assuming one explanation.
02 · RECOGNISE THE SIGNS

What lifting can look like

  • A visible gap or pocket between product and nail.
  • An edge that catches or appears raised.
  • Moisture or debris trapped beneath a separated area.
  • Cracks, breaks or product movement.
  • Repeated separation in a similar zone across services.
Do not press, glue down or seal over a lifted area. First assess the condition and follow the system's safe removal and maintenance guidance.
03 · PREPARATION

Start with the natural nail

Consistent, careful preparation supports adhesion. It must never compromise the natural nail.

  • Assess the nail and surrounding skin before starting.
  • Perform the preparation steps required by the selected product system.
  • Remove only non-living cuticle tissue as permitted by your training and local rules.
  • Remove dust and contaminants using the system-approved method.
  • Use only compatible prep products and allow them to be used as directed.
Avoid aggressive buffing, over-filing, skin injury or applying product over contamination. Never treat damaged or potentially infected nails as a routine service.
04 · PRODUCT CONTROL

Keep product where it belongs

  • Choose products suitable for the service and natural nail.
  • Follow the manufacturer's application and layer guidance.
  • Keep uncured product off skin and surrounding tissue.
  • Avoid flooding the cuticle and sidewall areas.
  • Do not mix systems unless compatibility is confirmed by the manufacturer.
05 · CURING

Use the correct lamp and schedule

A product that looks dry on the surface is not necessarily fully cured.

  • Use the lamp specified or approved for the product system.
  • Follow the manufacturer's exact curing schedule.
  • Apply the recommended layer thickness.
  • Position the hand correctly and check thumb placement.
  • Do not guess, shorten the schedule or substitute an unapproved lamp.
If you suspect under-curing, stop and follow the manufacturer's safety and removal instructions. Never cure uncured gel on skin.
06 · POSSIBLE CONTRIBUTING FACTORS

Look beyond one cause

PREPARATION

Residual dust, oils, moisture or incomplete preparation can affect adhesion.

APPLICATION

Product touching skin, unsuitable thickness or incorrect application may contribute.

SYSTEM

Incompatible products, lamp mismatch or incorrect curing can create problems.

WEAR & LIFESTYLE

Impact, picking, using nails as tools or prolonged exposure may affect wear.

These are possible contributors, not a diagnosis. Record what you observe and review the product instructions and service process.
07 · TROUBLESHOOTING

Investigate the pattern

Ask what happened, where it happened and whether it repeats.

  • Which nail or area lifted: proximal, sidewall or free edge?
  • When did the separation first appear?
  • Was there an impact, picking or other event?
  • Was the same product system and approved lamp used?
  • Were preparation, application and cure steps followed as directed?
  • Does the client report discomfort, redness, swelling or other concerning symptoms?
Use a calm, non-blaming conversation. Keep factual notes and avoid making medical diagnoses.
08 · SAFE RESPONSE

What to do when lifting is found

  1. Pause the service and inspect the area without pulling or prying.
  2. Do not glue, press down or seal a lifted pocket.
  3. Follow the manufacturer's safe removal or maintenance instructions.
  4. Do not apply fresh product over separated, contaminated or compromised material.
  5. If the natural nail or surrounding skin appears damaged, stop and advise appropriate professional assessment.
Never diagnose or work over a potentially infectious concern. Follow your local professional scope and refer the client to an appropriate healthcare professional when needed.
09 · PREVENTION ROUTINE

Build a repeatable service check

  • Inspect nails and skin before every service.
  • Prepare gently and consistently.
  • Use compatible products and approved equipment.
  • Control product placement and layer thickness.
  • Follow exact cure schedules.
  • Explain aftercare and discourage picking or using nails as tools.
  • Record recurring issues and review the process.
10 · PRACTICE ACTIVITY

Build a lifting investigation record

Use a fictional case study or training example. Do not experiment on a client’s damaged nail.

  1. Describe the visible sign and exact location of separation.
  2. List three possible contributing factors without assuming a cause.
  3. Write the safe immediate action you would take.
  4. Note which manufacturer instructions or system details you would check.
  5. Write one prevention step to review before the next service.
Practice goal

Observe carefully, respond safely and document facts—not assumptions.
